# Generalization as Inductive Structure

## From ARC to a structural account of epiplexity

### Abstract

A common way to discuss generalization is to assign candidate explanations a
scalar complexity and prefer the lowest-cost consistent one. Epiplexity takes a
different operational route: structure is probed through a learning experiment.
Both approaches leave open a more basic question: **what is the mathematical
object that is being discovered when a learner generalizes?**

This note develops an alternative starting point. Generalization is represented
by a **partial order of explanations**, generated by structural relations such
as substitution, anti-unification, parameter freeing, and constraint weakening.
Evidence filters explanations but need not totally order them. Genuine ambiguity
is therefore preserved. A fixed inductive representation induces a consequence
or closure relation; learning changes that representation. This separates the
**semantic endpoint of generalization** from the **dynamics and cost of
discovering it**.

A real ARC task, `7e0986d6`, illustrates the distinction. The official training
and test examples support both a connected-component explanation and a per-cell
shortcut. A six-cell counterexample distinguishes them. An incremental learner
presented with the same three demonstrations in all six possible orders reaches
the same final inductive representation, while discovering the crucial
component abstraction at different points in the sequence. This suggests a
possible structural interpretation of what learning experiments reveal, and a
possible role for epiplexity-like quantities: not as the definition of
structure, but as measures of the difficulty of discovering an underlying
inductive structure.

---

## 1. Motivation

Suppose a finite set of examples admits many programs. Selecting the shortest
program is one possible inductive principle, but it forces all relevant
preferences through a scalar cost. In ARC this is especially uncomfortable:
two explanations may be incomparable for a good reason, while a third may be an
obvious specialization of one of them.

The starting proposal is deliberately weak:

> Do not rank two explanations unless there is a justified structural relation
> between them.

Let `H` be a hypothesis space and write

$$h_1 \preceq h_2$$

when `h2` is at least as general as `h1`. The relation need only be a preorder;
a partial order is obtained after quotienting equivalent hypotheses.

Given evidence `E`, define the version space

$$V(E)=\{h\in H \mid h\text{ agrees with }E\}.$$

The candidate generalizations are the undominated consistent hypotheses:

$$G(E)=\mathrm{Max}_{\preceq}\,V(E).$$

There need not be one element of `G(E)`.

This is a feature rather than a defect: if the evidence and structural theory do
not distinguish two explanations, the formalism should not manufacture a
winner.

---

## 2. First source of order: weakening assumptions

Consider explanations with the same behavioral body but different applicability
conditions:

```text
Largest;X
Largest&Leftmost;X
```

Every object selected by `Largest&Leftmost` is selected by `Largest`, but not
conversely. Thus the second explanation is a semantic restriction of the first.

More generally, write an explanation as

$$(t,\Phi)$$

where `t` is a parametric term and `Phi` constrains its parameters or domain.
For the same term,

$$(t,\Phi_1) \succeq (t,\Phi_2)$$

when

$$\Phi_2 \Rightarrow \Phi_1.$$

Generality is therefore **constraint weakening**. No number of assumptions is
counted; implication supplies the order.

This subsumes examples such as

```text
Largest&Leftmost  ->  Largest
FlipX(size in {2,3,4})  ->  FlipX(any size)
```

when the relevant parameter domain is already part of the representation.

---

## 3. Anti-unification constructs new generality structure

Constraint weakening assumes that the parametric term already exists. Learning
can also construct it.

From ground terms

```text
Flip(X,2)
Flip(X,3)
Flip(X,4)
```

first-order anti-unification produces

```text
Flip(X,n)
```

with the observations factored as

$$t_i=T\sigma_i.$$

Before discovering `T`, the observations can be unrelated ground facts. After
discovering `T`, they are instances of a common schema.

This suggests an elementary structural learning event:

> **discover a factorization of several observations into a shared schema and
> varying substitutions.**

Counterexamples can subsequently show that a variable was too permissive. For
example, anti-unifying both action and size may produce

```text
Flip(a,n)
```

which can later be split into

```text
Flip(X,n)
Flip(Y,n)
```

while retaining the useful size parameter.

Thus representation learning has two complementary operations:

```text
merge distinctions that do not matter
split distinctions that do matter
```

Neither direction is globally preferable.

---

## 4. Variables require domains and relations

Anti-unification alone does not justify arbitrary substitution.

From

```text
Flip(X,2), Flip(X,3), Flip(X,4)
```

we can discover a size variable, but predicting size 5 requires knowing what
values that variable may take.

There are importantly different sources of such constraints:

1. **background typing**, e.g. `n : ObjectSize`;
2. **semantic constraints of primitives**, e.g. `FlipX` preserves dimensions;
3. **empirically induced relations**, e.g. `b = a + 1` inferred from observed
   substitutions `(2,3), (3,4), (4,5)`.

A learned schema is therefore naturally

$$(t,\Phi)$$

where `Phi` describes admissible substitutions.

Different representations expose different constraint languages. A ground
representation may express only the observed pairs. An interval abstraction may
admit a rectangular region and overgeneralize. A relational abstraction may
express `b-a=1` and capture the intended family exactly.

This is one point of contact with abstract interpretation: the choice of
abstract domain determines which relationships can be represented.

---

## 5. Closure is the semantics of a fixed inductive representation

Let `G` be a finite universe of ground instances. A representation `R` determines
which instances follow from which observations. Extensionally, this can often be
represented by a closure operator

$$cl_R : P(G) \to P(G).$$

For fixed `R`, the usual closure properties are appropriate:

$$S\subseteq cl_R(S),$$

$$S\subseteq U \Rightarrow cl_R(S)\subseteq cl_R(U),$$

$$cl_R(cl_R(S))=cl_R(S).$$

The important correction is:

> **closure is not learning. Closure is the consequence semantics of a fixed
> inductive state. Learning changes the inductive state.**

Thus a learning trajectory is better represented as

$$R_0\to R_1\to\cdots\to R_k$$

with corresponding consequence structures

$$cl_{R_0}\to cl_{R_1}\to\cdots\to cl_{R_k}.$$

Predictions need not grow monotonically across this sequence because a new
example may cause the representation itself to change.

---

## 6. Soundness, completeness, and exact generalization

For a synthetic task where the intended concept `T` is known and observations
satisfy `E subset T`, define:

**Inductive soundness**

$$cl_R(E)\subseteq T.$$

**Inductive completeness**

$$T\subseteq cl_R(E).$$

**Exactness**

$$cl_R(E)=T.$$

These give two different representation errors without a scalar metric:

```text
undergeneralization: closure is too small
overgeneralization:  closure extends outside the target
```

A useful representation refinement may therefore move in either direction. It
may merge distinctions to repair undergeneralization or split an abstraction to
repair overgeneralization.

The natural pointwise order on closure operators describes aggressiveness of
generalization, not quality. More closure is not automatically better.

## 7. Representation matters because it changes the order

Consider the same family of ground transformations represented in two ways.

Pixel-level:

```text
Flip2
Flip3
Flip4
```

Object-level:

```text
FlipX(2)
FlipX(3)
FlipX(4)
```

At the pixel level the three operations may be unrelated atoms. At the object
level they anti-unify immediately to

```text
FlipX(n)
```

and therefore participate in an instantiation order.

The observations and ground behavior have not changed. The **generality
structure has changed**.

This gives a precise interpretation of the claim that abstraction helps
generalization:

> An abstraction can expose substitution, subsumption, or constraint relations
> between concrete explanations that were absent in the lower-level
> representation.

This effect is independent of search efficiency. An abstraction may make search
cheaper without inducing the right generalization structure, or expose the right
inductive structure even if search cost is ignored.

## 8. A real ARC case: task 7e0986d6

The task contains large regions of one foreground color disturbed by small
components of another color. The output removes the nuisance components,
restoring nuisance cells to the nearby large component's color when they belong
to it and otherwise returning them to background.

The two official training examples use different foreground colors, and the
test changes them again. Thus fixed color identities are directly refuted by the
evidence.

We compared four executable explanations:

```text
structural
    connected-component repair; colors are parameters

frequency_shortcut
    same component repair plus assumptions that nuisance/reference roles are
    global frequency min/max

fixed_first_pair
    same repair with colors fixed to the first training example

per_cell_local
    choose color roles by frequency, then classify each nuisance pixel
    independently from its immediate neighbors
```

The actual result was:

```text
candidate                 train   test
structural                True    True
frequency_shortcut        True    True
fixed_first_pair          False   False
per_cell_local            True    True
```

The justified order among training-consistent explanations contains

```text
structural > frequency_shortcut
```

because the latter is the same component schema plus additional assumptions.
The fixed-color explanation is eliminated by evidence.

Crucially,

```text
structural || per_cell_local
```

under the current structural theory. Both fit every official training and test
example. The partial order correctly refuses to invent a preference.

This is a concrete example where interpolation plus the official held-out test
still leaves distinct explanatory schemas unresolved.

## 9. A six-cell example reveals the missing abstraction

An exhaustive small-grid search found the following minimal meaningful witness:

```text
input       structural output       per-cell output

RRR         RRR                     RRR
Rnn         RRR                     RR.
```

`R` is the reference color and `n` the nuisance color.

The two nuisance cells form one connected component. Collectively the component
is sufficiently adjacent to the reference component, so the structural rule
repairs both cells. The per-cell rule treats them independently and erases the
rightmost cell.

Adding the structural output as a new demonstration preserves the component
explanation and refutes the per-cell explanation.

The striking point is that this **six-foreground-cell example exposes a
structural distinction that the two much larger ARC demonstrations and the
official test did not expose**.

The value of an example is therefore not well represented by its number of
cells or raw data size. Its value depends on which ambiguity in the current
inductive state it resolves.

## 10. Confluence versus curriculum

Let the two official demonstrations be `A` and `B`, and the six-cell witness be
`W`.

We implemented an incremental learner whose state records three independently
revisable commitments:

1. fixed versus parametric color roles;
2. per-cell versus connected-component structure;
3. an extra frequency-based role assumption.

The learner does not recompute the batch version space after each observation.
It locally repairs its current representation only when evidence forces a
change.

All six permutations of `A`, `B`, and `W` were run. They all reached exactly the
same final representation and semantic state:

```text
parametric colors
connected nuisance components
no frequency-role assumption
```

Thus, for this learner and this example set:

```text
strong confluence:   true
semantic confluence: true
```

But the trajectories differ substantially.

For curriculum

```text
A -> B -> W
```

the learner first keeps fixed colors, then anti-unifies colors, and only at the
third example discovers connected-component structure.

For

```text
W -> A -> B
```

component structure is discovered immediately at the first example; later
examples primarily establish color parametricity.

Therefore:

> **the final inductive semantics can be order-independent while the discovery
> trajectory is strongly curriculum-dependent.**

The implementation printed the confluence summary successfully; a subsequent
assertion failed only because it indexed the human-readable trace incorrectly,
not because the confluence property failed.

## 11. Revelation is state-relative

An example does not carry a fixed amount of structural information independently
of the learner's current state.

Let `I` be an inductive state and let

$$Rev_I(e)$$

denote the representation changes forced or enabled by observing `e` from
state `I`.

After observing `A`, the large official example `B` reveals approximately

```text
{ color parametricity }
```

whereas the tiny witness `W` reveals

```text
{ color parametricity,
  connected-component coupling,
  removal of the frequency assumption }
```

for the incremental learner used in the experiment.

This suggests a state-relative partial order on examples:

$$e_1 \preceq_I e_2$$

when

$$Rev_I(e_1)\subseteq Rev_I(e_2).$$

No numerical information value is required. Two examples may also reveal
incomparable structural facts.

More generally, a learning event is an interval or transition

$$I\longrightarrow I'$$

in a partially ordered space of inductive states.

## 12. Relation to established ideas

Much of the machinery has classical antecedents, which is useful rather than
problematic.

**Anti-unification.** Plotkin and Reynolds introduced least-general
generalization by substitution. It provides a natural mechanism for constructing
parametric schemas from ground explanations.

**Version spaces.** Mitchell's version-space framework keeps all hypotheses
consistent with observations under a general-to-specific partial order. This is
close to retaining the undominated elements of `V(E)` rather than forcing a
single winner.

**Inductive Logic Programming.** Theta-subsumption and semantic generality
provide mature accounts of generalization/specialization relative to structured
background knowledge.

**Programming by example.** Version-space algebras and related synthesis
techniques compactly represent many programs consistent with sparse examples;
the unresolved problem of intended extrapolation is directly relevant here.

**Abstract interpretation.** Closure operators and abstract domains provide an
order-theoretic language for representations and their refinements. The
orientation here is inductive rather than analytic: the question is which
representation induces the right extrapolation from sparse evidence.

These traditions suggest that the interesting question is not whether the
individual mathematical pieces are new, but whether they give a useful unified
account of generalization and learning dynamics.

## 13. Connection to epiplexity

The investigation began from a concern about defining learnable structure by
training a model. The experiments suggest a possible separation.

The underlying structural object may be an **inductive representation**: a
preorder/consequence structure saying which explanations are instances,
specializations, or consequences of others. A closure operator can give its
extensional semantics.

A learning experiment then probes the computational process by which an observer
constructs or refines that representation:

$$I_0\to I_1\to\cdots\to I_*.$$

Different curricula may reach the same `I*` through very different paths. The
ARC confluence experiment gives a microscopic example: all six curricula reach
the same final representation, but the decisive component abstraction is
learned at step 1 in some orders and step 3 in others.
